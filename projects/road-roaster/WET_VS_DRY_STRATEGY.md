## 1. Executive Summary & The Core Thermodynamic Trade-Off

A central question in thermal hardscape management is whether the target gravel bed should be **dry** or **pre-hydrated/damp** before operating the Road Roaster.

Pre-wetting gravel creates a fundamental thermodynamic tension between **two opposing physical mechanisms**:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                          THE WET vs. DRY PHYSICAL TENSION                              │
├────────────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────┤

│                                        │                                               │
│ • Water replaces insulating air gaps   │ • Water has 5× the specific heat of stone     │
│   between stones (4× to 6× higher k).  │   (1.00 vs 0.20 BTU/lb·°F).                   │
│ • Steam expands into cracks and dumps  │ • Vaporizing water consumes an enormous       │
│   970 BTU/lb of latent heat upon       │   970.3 BTU/lb of energy before leaf cells    │
│   condensing onto root crowns.         │   can reach lethal shock temperatures.        │
```

**The Core Operational Rule:**
* **Run DRY** for **Fast Glide Foliar Eradication** (surface broadleaf seedlings, annual grasses, general driveway maintenance at 1–2 mph).
* **Run LIGHTLY DAMP** for **Deep Heat-Soak Station Dwells** (perennial taproots like dandelions/thistles, woody root crowns, and dormant sub-surface seed banks).

## 2. The Physics of Soil & Gravel Moisture

### 2.1 Thermal Conductivity & The "Air Void" Problem
Dry gravel consists of solid mineral aggregate separated by air-filled interstitial voids. Because stagnant dry air is an exceptional thermal insulator ($k \approx 0.026\text{ W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$), dry gravel acts like an insulating blanket: the top stones become red hot, while the soil just 1 inch below remains cool.

When gravel is lightly damp, water ($k \approx 0.60\text{ W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$) displaces the air pockets, forming liquid thermal bridges across stone contact points.

| Medium / State | Thermal Conductivity ($k$) | Specific Heat ($c_p$) | Energy to Heat 10 lbs by 100°F |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dry Air (Pores)** | $0.026\text{ W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$ | $0.24\text{ BTU/(lb}\cdot^\circ\text{F)}$ | N/A (Insulator) |
| **Dry Granite / Quartz Gravel** | $0.25 - 0.40\text{ W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$ | **$0.20\text{ BTU/(lb}\cdot^\circ\text{F)}$** | **$200\text{ BTU}$** |
| **Damp Gravel Matrix (10% moisture)** | **$1.50 - 2.50\text{ W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$** | $0.28\text{ BTU/(lb}\cdot^\circ\text{F)}$ | **$280\text{ BTU}$** |
| **Pure Liquid Water** | $0.60\text{ W/(m}\cdot\text{K)}$ | **$1.00\text{ BTU/(lb}\cdot^\circ\text{F)}$** | **$1,000\text{ BTU}$** |
| **Water Phase Change (Boiling to Steam)** | N/A | **$h_{fg} = 970.3\text{ BTU/lb}$** | **$9,703\text{ BTU}$** |

### 2.2 The Latent Heat Steam Condensation Mechanism

When the Road Roaster's enclosed hood ($14\text{-gauge steel}$ with $2.0''$ perimeter skirts) sits over damp gravel during a stationary dwell:

```
                      [ 1,600°F Overhead Ceramic Broiler ]
                                       │
                                       ▼ (Radiant Flux)
                      ┌─────────────────────────────────┐
                      │    Enclosed Steam Pressure      │
                      │    Chamber (Under Hood Skirts)  │
                      └────────────────┬────────────────┘
                                       │
                ▼                      ▼                      ▼
    [ Flash Micro-Steam ]    [ Steam Expansion ]    [ Interstice Permeation ]
    (Surface moisture        (Trapped by skirts,    (Steam drives 1–2" down
     flashes at 212°F)        expands into gravel)   into soil pores)
                                       │
                                       ▼
    =========================================================================
    [ LATENT HEAT DUMP UPON CONDENSATION ]
    When steam reaches cooler sub-surface soil (140°F–160°F), it CONDENSES,
    instantly releasing 970 BTU/lb of latent heat directly into root crowns!
    =========================================================================
```

1. **Surface Flash**: The intense infrared flux instantly boils interstitial water in the top $1/2''$ into micro-steam.
2. **Pneumatic Permeation**: Because the perimeter skirts restrict horizontal exhaust, the expanding steam is forced downward through the gravel matrix into the sub-base soil.
3. **Latent Heat Release**: As the steam hits the cooler sub-surface root zone ($120^\circ\text{F} - 150^\circ\text{F}$), it **condenses back into liquid water**, releasing **$970.3\text{ BTU}$ of energy per pound of steam directly into the plant tissue**.
4. **Lethal Root Cooking**: This cooks taproots and seed coats uniformly from all sides via saturated condensation, completely avoiding the directional shadows of dry radiation.

### Strategy A: Dry Gravel (Fast Glide Foliar Eradication)

#### When to Use:
* Routine bi-weekly or monthly driveway corridor maintenance.
* Sprouting annual weeds, crabgrass seedlings, young broadleaf shoots (<2" tall).
* Moss and lichen on dry pavers and flagstone.

#### Why Dry is Mandatory for Speed:
* **No Water Shielding**: On dry weeds, 100% of incoming photons from the overhead ceramic broiler hit the leaf cuticle and chlorophyll directly. Intracellular sap is brought to $160^\circ\text{F}$ in under 1 second.
* **Fuel Efficiency**: Zero BTUs are wasted evaporating surface moisture films or puddle water.
* **Walking Pace**: Enables continuous forward walking speed ($1.0 - 1.5\text{ mph}$), treating thousands of square feet per hour on a single 1 lb propane canister.

---

### Strategy B: Lightly Damp Gravel (Deep Root & Seed Bank Sterilization)

#### When to Use:
* Deep-rooted perennial invasives (Bull Thistle, Canada Thistle, Dandelion, Field Bindweed, Horsetail).
* Heavy gravel beds where weeds repeatedly regenerate from deep subterranean crowns.
* End-of-season seed bank devitalization to prevent spring germination.

#### Field Pre-Hydration Procedure:
1. **Light Mist Only**: Use a garden hose with a fine mist nozzle or backpack sprayer to lightly wet the gravel **10 to 15 minutes before treatment**.
2. **Moisture Target**: The surface should appear damp/darkened, but with **zero standing puddles or pooling water**. The 10-minute wait allows moisture to percolate into the sand/gravel pore network between the rocks.
3. **Stationary Dwell Execution**:
   * Roll the Road Roaster directly over the root crown cluster.
   * Drop the hood flat on the skids.
   * Hold the heat position for **15 to 30 seconds**.
   * Observe steam venting gently from the perimeter skirt edges.
4. **Result**: The sub-surface gravel matrix will reach $180^\circ\text{F} - 200^\circ\text{F}$ down to a depth of $1.5'' - 2.0''$, permanently devitalizing the taproot.

## 5. Architectural & Design Implications for Road Roaster

1. **Perimeter Skirt Sealing**: The $2.0''$ vertical steel skirts on the Road Roaster hood are essential for capturing and pressurizing the micro-steam plume during damp dwell operations, preventing steam from escaping horizontally into ambient air.
2. **Corrosion Resistance**: Because steam condensation occurs under the hood during damp heat-soak operations, internal structural welds and the ceramic burner mounting bracket should be coated with high-temperature ceramic exhaust paint (rated $2,000^\circ\text{F}$) or fabricated from 304 stainless steel.
3. **Dual-Mode Workflow**: The Road Roaster's mechanical chassis—with solid rear wheels and quick-release skid engagement—naturally supports both modes: fast continuous towing across dry stretches, and instant stopping for damp spot-dwell sterilization.
